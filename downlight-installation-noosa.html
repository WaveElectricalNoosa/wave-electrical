LED Downlights Done Right

More Than Just Changing a Globe

Downlight installation looks simple from the outside, but there are a lot of ways to get it wrong. The spacing is off and you get patches of light and dark. The colour temperature is too warm or too cool for the room. The trims don't sit flush. The driver hums. We've fixed plenty of DIY and budget-job downlight work over the years — it's more involved than it looks.

When Wave Electrical installs downlights, we get the layout right before we cut any holes. We recommend the right colour temperature and beam angle for each room. We make sure the fittings are IC-rated where they need to be (ceiling insulation contact), and we install everything cleanly so the finished result looks intentional, not patched.

Whether you're swapping out old halogens, adding downlights to a new room, or planning a full lighting package for a renovation — we've done it hundreds of times across Noosa and know what works.

Common Questions

Downlight FAQ — Noosa

Can I swap halogen downlights for LED myself?
For plug-in GU10 halogens (no wiring involved), a competent person can swap the globe. But if the fitting itself needs replacing, or there's any wiring involved, it must be done by a licensed electrician in Queensland. We handle it correctly — and often the old fittings aren't worth keeping anyway.
What colour temperature should I choose?
It depends on the room and the look you're after. 2700K–3000K (warm white) suits bedrooms, living rooms and bathrooms where you want a relaxed feel. 4000K (neutral white) suits kitchens and workspaces where you want clarity without the cold feel of daylight globes. We'll recommend what works for your space.
What does IC-rated mean and do I need it?
IC stands for Insulation Contact — an IC-rated downlight can have insulation placed over it without fire risk. If your ceiling has insulation (or will have it added), you need IC-rated downlights. We check this as standard before any installation.
How many downlights do I need per room?
As a rule of thumb, one downlight per 1.5–2m² for general lighting, but it depends on beam angle, ceiling height, and whether you have supplementary task lighting. We'll work out the right spacing for each room so you don't end up with dark spots or blinding brightness.
Do you supply the downlights or do I need to buy them?
We can supply quality LED downlights as part of the job, or install fittings you've purchased yourself. If you're supplying them, check they're IC-rated and dimmer-compatible before you buy — we can advise on what to look for.
